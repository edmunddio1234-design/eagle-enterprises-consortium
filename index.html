What the Consortium Is

An integrated “one-stop” model for rural empowerment

Many rural veterans and underserved community members face fragmented support systems and limited access to resources. National research shows that 39% of veteran entrepreneurs live in rural areas where support networks are sparse, and nearly half of rural veterans report difficulty navigating resources — 65% versus 46% overall. These gaps underscore the need for an integrated “one-stop” model, and that model is what Eagle Enterprises Consortium was built to coordinate.

EEC is a workforce-development and entrepreneurial-training consortium built on a participant-run business model — launched circa 2019 with an initial cohort of roughly fourteen students and participants. Its mission: to coordinate rural-focused veteran services and community development through integrated social enterprise initiatives.

Operating under one umbrella lets the consortium’s interrelated entities share resources, cross-refer participants, and present unified outreach. Each entity addresses a facet of empowerment — immediate personal crisis support, entrepreneurial training and social enterprise, workforce development, an innovation complex, disaster resiliency, and provider engagement — while EEC supplies unified leadership and shared services: administration, communications, and evaluation.

The opportunity is real. Veteran-led firms have lower failure rates and higher five-year survival than average; veterans accounted for 8% of new U.S. businesses in 2023, up from 5% in 2022, and over half of veteran entrepreneurs start community-oriented businesses — restaurants, local services, the lifeblood of rural towns.

Siloed but Connected

The four-entity ecosystem EEC coordinates

Each entity stands on its own legal and operational feet. EEC keeps them pulling in the same direction.

Eagle Enterprises Consortium, L3C — the umbrella

Coordinates the network, aligns mission with sustainable social enterprise, and maintains accountability across the ecosystem. Houses Net-WIRCing Solutions, LLC and the Mobile Triage Outreach Cavalry (M-TOC™).

Lighthouse Community Development Corporation (LCDC)

The 501(c)(3)-aligned community development corporation — transitional housing pathways, benefits navigation, and coordinated direct services. LCDC leads real estate and infrastructure, including housing and the Innovation Complex. Visit the Lighthouse main site.

RV3 — Rural Veterans Vanguard of Valor Foundation

The philanthropic arm. Honors service and channels donor and grant support to rural veteran programs across the network. Visit the RV3 site.

Guardian Angels Enterprises, L3C

The advocacy, volunteers & communications hub — website, app, podcast, newsletter, blog, and social media — mobilizing volunteers in support of veterans. Service, honor, legacy. Visit the Guardian Angels site.

Implementation Roadmap

From front door to self-sustaining network

The consortium’s master plan phases the build deliberately — start with triage and outreach, grow into incubation and capital, and institutionalize by Year 5.

First 90 days — communications live

Website and social channels launched; outreach calendar set. Communications, advocacy and outreach are a named strategic goal domain from day one.

By month 6 — the front door opens

Mobile Triage Unit established as the “front door” for rural communities; pilot entrepreneurship training launched; first podcast episodes of Operation: Rural Veterans’ Renaissance and Reparations released; quarterly outreach events begin.

Year 1 — incubation takes a building

Physical culinary incubator opens; training tracks expand to TOWED trades and agribusiness; the annual Rural Entrepreneurship Bootcamp (modeled on Syracuse’s EBV) is established.

Years 1–3 — case management and capital

Full case management built across the network; CHDO designation pursued by Year 2 and CDFI status or partnership by Year 3 to unlock housing and community-lending capital; performance dashboards mature.

Year 5 — a self-sustaining model

Diversified revenue — grants, donations, sponsorships, and earned income — supports a self-sustaining consortium ready for statewide and, eventually, national replication.
