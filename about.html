Background

The population we serve — and the gap we close

The consortium — encompassing EEC, LCDC, Café Kountry Gumbeaux, the Rural Culinary Incubator, the Mobile Triage Unit, the Innovation Complex, CAPE™, the Disaster Management & Resiliency Center, and TOWED — is a multi-faceted initiative aimed at empowering rural veterans, aspiring entrepreneurs, underserved populations, and community stakeholders. It takes a holistic approach to personal development, economic enrichment, community resilience, and organizational sustainability, with an initial focus on Louisiana’s Florida Parishes and a vision to expand statewide and nationally.

Many rural veterans and underserved members face fragmented support systems and limited access to resources. 39% of veteran entrepreneurs live in rural areas where support networks are sparse; 65% of rural veterans report difficulty navigating resources versus 46% overall.

Yet the opportunity in veteran entrepreneurship is equally clear: veteran-led firms have lower failure rates and higher five-year survival than average; veterans accounted for 8% of new U.S. businesses in 2023 (up from 5% in 2022); and over half of veteran entrepreneurs start community-oriented businesses — restaurants, local services — the exact enterprises rural towns need most.

The consortium remains in an active concept-to-execution phase: the 2026 LCDC + EEC Strategic Plan and Work Plan converts the founding framework into quarterly targets with named owners, measurable KPIs, and data sources for performance review.

The Consortium Concept

Interrelated entities, one referral system

Each entity addresses a facet of empowerment. Operating under one umbrella lets them share resources, cross-refer participants, and present unified outreach.

Mobile Triage (M-TOC™)

Immediate personal crisis support — the network’s front door.

Café Kountry Gumbeaux & the Rural Culinary Incubator

Entrepreneurial training and social enterprise, anchored in food.

TOWED — Technical Opportunities & Workplace Empowerment Division

Workforce development: trades, technical skills, and employment pathways.

The Innovation Complex (RDIC)

A training and co-working hub supplying capital access, workspace, and entrepreneurial support.

CAPE™ — Comprehensive Assessment, Planning & Empowerment

Partnerships, communications, the podcast, and warm hand-offs across the provider network.

Disaster Management & Resiliency Center

Disaster readiness for rural communities — including certifying veterans as volunteer firefighters and EMTs.

How a journey flows: a veteran may start at Mobile Triage → move into TOWED job training and Incubator workshops → then launch a venture the Strategic Investment team helps fund. One referral system connects it all.

Governance

Why an L3C — and how the consortium is governed

What an L3C is

A low-profit limited liability company (L3C) is a legal structure built for exactly this work: it blends a primary charitable or educational mission with the operating discipline and earned-revenue capacity of a business. An L3C must significantly further a social purpose — profit is permitted, but it is the means, never the point. For EEC, that means subscription revenue, consulting contracts, and enterprise income can sustain veteran services without waiting on the next grant cycle.

EEC’s registered home is 16458 Highway 38, Greensburg, Louisiana 70441-3602 — in the heart of the Florida Parishes it serves first.

The two-entity operating model

Consortium leadership — EEC and LCDC together — forms the executive and fiduciary core with a Governing Board. LCDC, the community development entity, leads stabilization, real estate, and infrastructure: housing navigation, wraparound supports, benefits access, and coordinated referrals. EEC (L3C) leads earned-income, entrepreneurship, and enterprise capacity building: veteran-led and mission-aligned social enterprises, consulting and referral services, and workforce pathways.

Standing committees keep the machine honest: Equity and Access; Programs and Service Delivery; Enterprise and Economic Mobility (EEC); Development; Finance and Audit; and Data, Evaluation, and Learning — with monthly committee meetings, cross-pollination memos, and a quarterly executive review of scorecard, work plan, and budget-to-actual.

Leadership

Founder-led, board-governed, committee-driven

Fredell Butler, founder, has carried this vision from LCDC’s community-development roots (circa 2003) and EEC’s first participant-run cohort (circa 2019, ~14 students and participants) into today’s four-entity ecosystem. The consortium’s motto is his operating creed: “Grateful for the opportunity. Committed to the responsibility. Devoted to the legacy.”

Default owner roles across the 2026 work plan include an Executive Director (LCDC), a Managing Director/President (EEC), a shared COO/Director of Operations, a Director of Veterans Affairs / Outreach & Triage Lead, a Housing Development Lead / CHDO Liaison, a Capital Access & Economic Mobility Lead / CDFI Liaison, plus development, finance, communications, data, and HR/equity leads.

Accountability by design

  • Monthly committee meetings with one-page updates.
  • Every-other-month cross-pollination memos between committees.
  • Quarterly executive review: scorecard, work-plan progress, budget-to-actual, and corrective actions.
  • Annual strategy refresh and target-setting.
  • A unified metrics dashboard covering service delivery, housing stabilization, claims navigation, partner performance, enterprise pipeline, and fundraising.

Implementation Phases

Concept → pilot → scale → sustain

Phase 1

Stand up the core

Website and social within 90 days; Mobile Triage front door and pilot training within six months; first podcast episodes; quarterly outreach events begin.

Phase 2

Open the doors

Physical culinary incubator by Year 1; TOWED trades and agribusiness tracks; the annual Rural Entrepreneurship Bootcamp launches.

Phase 3

Unlock capital

CHDO designation by Year 2; CDFI status or partnership by Year 3 to unlock housing and community-lending capital; performance dashboards mature.

Phase 4

Sustain & replicate

Self-sustaining model by Year 5; replication across rural Louisiana, then regional and national scaling through partnerships and licensing pathways.
