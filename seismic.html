The Method

Incubation the Lean Impact way

SEISMIC is the consortium’s second strategic goal domain in action — Enrichment, Education & Entrepreneurship: skill-building, social enterprise development, entrepreneurial coaching, nonprofit startup guidance, and incubators. The plan called for pilot training within six months and a physical culinary incubator by Year 1, with tracks expanding into TOWED trades and agribusiness.

The methodology comes straight from the consortium’s reference canon: hypothesis-driven, test-and-iterate program design rather than plan-and-pray. Start with the simplest prototype, learn from real-world response, iterate, then invest — because innovation is the path and impact is the destination.

Three guiding principles

  • Think big — base goals on the size of the real need, not what seems incrementally achievable.
  • Start small — resist scaling too soon; small starts make it easier to learn and adapt.
  • Relentlessly seek impact — fall in love with the problem, not your solution.

Every SEISMIC venture gets the same discipline: a mission brief, a service blueprint, a funding stack, a KPI scorecard, and a 90-day sprint plan.

Flagship Food Enterprises

From gumbo pot to business plan

Social Enterprise

Café Kountry Gumbeaux

A social-enterprise café and training kitchen offering culinary job training for veterans and incubating veteran-owned food businesses. The café is both a revenue engine and a classroom — every plate served funds and trains the next food entrepreneur.

Incubator

Rural Culinary Incubator

Entrepreneur training, commercial kitchen workspace, and a farm-to-table pipeline — developed with the Small Business Development Center at Southern University. Food start-ups get the affordable licensed kitchen space, food-safety guidance, and market channels they cannot find anywhere else in rural Louisiana.

The Community Dream Kitchen lineage

The concept began as East Feliciana’s first community food kitchen — the “Community Dream Kitchen” at 10413 Plank Road in Clinton, Louisiana — a restaurant property offered by longtime Clinton resident Mr. George Johnson, with proposed partners including the Southern University Ag Center, the East Feliciana Chamber of Commerce, the City of Clinton, and the Louisiana Small Business Development Center. Its mission: to be a stimulus for economic development through all-inclusive support of promising food entrepreneurs and farmers who provide food security to the heart of the rural community. Food entrepreneurs struggle to find affordable commercial kitchen space and to meet food-safety metrics and state mandates; the kitchen model supports catering, farmers markets and festivals, personal chefs, packaged goods, and food kiosks — helping “foodpreneurs” build sustainable, safe food businesses. That blueprint now anchors the culinary components of the Greensburg flagship site.

Workforce Division

TOWED — Technical Opportunities & Workplace Empowerment Division

TOWED is the consortium’s workforce-development division: trades training, technical skills, and employment pathways for veterans and underserved rural residents. In the consortium’s referral chain, TOWED is often the second stop — a veteran stabilized by M-TOC moves into TOWED job training and incubator workshops, then launches a venture the Strategic Investment team helps fund.

TOWED tracks grow alongside the incubator: skilled trades, agribusiness, disaster resiliency roles (including certifying veterans as volunteer firefighters and EMTs through the Disaster Management & Resiliency Center), and technology skills delivered with Net-WIRCing.

Workforce pathways include

  • Trades and technical certifications aligned to rural employer demand.
  • Agribusiness and farm-to-table production supporting the culinary pipeline.
  • Digital and IT skills cohorts with Net-WIRCing Solutions.
  • DoD SkillBridge-style transition pathways for service members in their final 180 days — real-world experience that converts to employment.
  • Volunteer firefighter and EMT certification for community resiliency.

Venture in Incubation

The nonprofit staffing agency & Café Eagles Nest

Among SEISMIC’s ventures-in-development is a nonprofit staffing agency whose vision is to bridge the talent gap in the nonprofit sector by connecting mission-driven professionals with purpose-aligned organizations — sourcing, screening, and placing candidates who align with each organization’s values.

Its signature event, Café Eagles Nest, runs quarterly on the second Saturday of the quarter’s second month: nonprofit providers submit job descriptions 30 days ahead, prospects submit resumes 30 days ahead, resumes go out to providers two weeks before, interview schedules lock one week out — and the event itself combines interviews, roundtables, and development stations.

Planned structure

  • Executive Director / CEO
  • Director of Nonprofit Relations
  • Director of Talent Recruitment
  • Event Coordinator (Café Eagles Nest lead)
  • HR & Compliance Manager
  • Administrative / Operations Support
  • Advisory: Nonprofit Advisory Council and Candidate Advisory Panel
